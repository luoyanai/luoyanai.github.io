Experience

Research Direction: Generative AI for Robotics & Embodied Robot Learning

World Models × Robot Learning × Physical Control

My research aims to build physically grounded, self-improving embodied robots that can learn from multimodal experience, reason about actions, adapt through reinforcement learning, and improve through real-world interaction.

I focus on integrating generative AI, video-based motion priors, multimodal foundation models, and world-model representations with reinforcement learning, control theory, and real-robot deployment. The central goal is to transform high-level visual, semantic, and generative knowledge into executable robot behaviors that are stable, robust, and embodiment-aware.

My representative work, MotionVL, develops a vision-language-supervised reinforcement learning framework for robot motion control. Instead of using foundation models only for perception, MotionVL uses them as semantic evaluators to analyze robot behaviors, generate structured feedback, and refine reward functions in a closed learning loop. The framework improves the success rate by 21.5 percentage points over manually designed reward baselines and has been deployed on real humanoid platforms for human-like upright walking.

Although my Ph.D. work includes humanoid locomotion, my broader research agenda is not limited to a single embodiment. I am interested in general robot learning across humanoids, mobile manipulators, collaborative arms, quadrupeds, and other physical agents. Going forward, I aim to develop robot learning systems that combine large-scale video priors, generative world models, embodiment-aware reinforcement learning, and real-world physical feedback to enable robots that can learn, act, diagnose failures, and continuously improve.

I have led multiple national and provincial robotics R&D projects as a principal investigator or technical lead, coordinating interdisciplinary teams across robot mechanism design, motion control, AI algorithms, and system integration.

My work includes the end-to-end development of humanoid and wheeled humanoid robotic systems, from system architecture and control software to algorithm deployment and real-world validation. This experience has shaped my system-level view of robot learning: algorithms should ultimately be evaluated through stable, robust, and deployable behavior on physical robots.

Several of these projects received external recognition, including national media coverage and selection for the Jiangsu Provincial Young Talent Support Program. More importantly, they strengthened my ability to connect research ideas with engineering execution and real-world robotic deployment.

Project Leadership & Real-Robot Systems

Technical Lead / System Integration

Standards, Safety & Reliable Deployment

T/CIET 648-2024

I contributed to the drafting of the industry standard “Humanoid Robot Technical Requirements” (T/CIET 648-2024), translating real-robot development experience into verifiable specifications for sensing performance, robustness, safety constraints, and system reliability.

This experience reinforced my view that deployable embodied intelligence requires more than model performance. It also depends on measurable safety boundaries, reproducible evaluation protocols, failure analysis, and robust behavior under real operating conditions.

Working with industrial and academic contributors on technical standardization gave me a broader perspective on how robotics research can move from laboratory demonstrations toward reliable, scalable, and safety-aware deployment.

Through sustained technical exchanges with robotics companies and AI hardware supply-chain partners, I have developed a deployment-oriented understanding of practical bottlenecks in embodied robotic systems, including reliability, cost, maintainability, hardware constraints, and scenario adaptability.

These interactions help me translate real industrial challenges into research questions and system designs, especially in areas such as real-robot validation, failure diagnosis, hardware-aware policy learning, and scalable deployment of embodied AI systems.

I have also delivered invited technical talks for professional associations and robotics communities, sharing research and engineering experience on humanoid robots, embodied intelligence, and industry-academia collaboration.

CoPickVLM: A Vision-Language Model Guided Dual-Arm Collaborative System for Occlusion-Aware Tomato Harvesting

Occlusion-aware recognition Tomato harvesting Dual-arm robot Vision-language model Collaborative manipulation Agricultural automation

This paper presents CoPickVLM, an occlusion-aware tomato recognition and dual-arm collaborative harvesting system based on vision-language model (VLM) reasoning. The system integrates occlusion-aware recognition and dual-arm coordination strategies. It decomposes harvesting into three semantic subtasks and applies a dual-arm control scheme where one arm removes occlusions and the other cuts.(Currently under revision.)

June 2025

MotionVL: Visual-Language Supervision for Guiding Reinforcement Learning in Humanoid Motion

Reinforcement learning Generative reward Large language models Multimodal supervision

This work introduces MotionVL, a novel framework for humanoid reinforcement learning that leverages multimodal large models to automate reward generation and semantic supervision. A Vision-Language Model encodes observations into natural language, while an LLM generates aligned reward functions. The closed-loop structure improves generalization and human-likeness. Experiments on straight-leg walking validate the method’s effectiveness over traditional handcrafted reward design.

March 7, 2025

DCM-based Dynamic Stable Walking under Terrain-Induced Time-Varying Disturbances for Humanoid Robots

Humanoid robot Dynamic balance control Capturability Time-varying disturbance

When humanoid robots walk on challenging terrain such as shaking platforms, time-varying disturbances can destabilize balance. This paper proposes a DCM-based time-varying disturbance walking (DCM-TVDW) method. It adjusts center of mass and stride height through variable height stabilization and integrates DCM with N-step capturability. This allows multi-step recovery from unstable states. Experiments show that the SJ-Bruce robot can walk stably on a platform with 22° inclination changes using this method.

A novel DCM-based time-varying disturbance walking framework is developed for humanoid robots facing abrupt terrain inclination changes. The method combines center-of-mass height regulation and N-step capturability to achieve dynamically stable walking, enabling successful traversal of shaking platforms with inclination angles up to 22°.
