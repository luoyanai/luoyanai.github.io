MotionVL

How can a robot learn a gait that a person can describe but would find hard to express as a reward? We use video of the robot’s own movement to guide the design of its next training reward.

Yan Luo, Jianhua Wu, Zhenhua Xiong, and Han Ding

Turning motion into a reward

A vision-language model describes the motion in a rollout. A language model reads that feedback alongside the requested movement and writes a reward function. We use the reward to train the policy with proximal policy optimization, then evaluate the resulting motion.

From simulation to hardware

We evaluated running, repeated jumping, and single-leg balance in simulation and deployed the straight-leg walking policy on a physical humanoid. Comparisons with hand-designed rewards and rewards generated from text alone tested what the visual feedback contributed.
